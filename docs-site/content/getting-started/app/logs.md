# Logs

Every trap keeps a running history of what it has done, so you can troubleshoot, monitor performance, and see exactly what happened and when. In the app this history is the trap's **Activity** feed.

## Where to find it

Open a trap and select the **Activity** tab on the trap detail page. Entries are grouped by day, newest first, and each row shows an icon, a short label, an optional detail message, and a timestamp.

To review activity across all of your traps at once, use the account-level **Activity** page, which combines events from every trap you own or manage.

## Filtering the feed

A row of chips at the top of the Activity tab lets you narrow the feed:

* **All:** every event
* **Images:** new captures and photos
* **Alerts:** pre-capture, temperature, battery, and device alerts
* **Heartbeats:** connectivity and check-in events (online/offline, cellular)
* **Commands:** remote commands you sent and their results
* **Settings:** settings changes

## What gets recorded

The Activity feed records the events that matter for operating and troubleshooting a trap, including:

* **Captures:** a new capture/detection, with its photo.
* **Remote commands:** commands sent from the app (open/close door, set arm mode, disarm & release, request image, request location, buzzer, reboot, sleep/hibernate), along with whether each command was confirmed or failed.
* **Arm-mode and settings changes:** when the trap is switched between **Unarmed**, **Scouting**, and **Armed**, and when device settings are changed.
* **Alerts:** pre-capture alerts, low-battery warnings, and high/low temperature alerts.
* **Connectivity:** when the trap goes online or offline and cellular connect/disconnect events.
* **Sharing:** when a trap is shared, a share is removed, or an invite is accepted.
* **Subscription and billing:** subscription and payment events, and ownership or billing transfers.

Errors and device faults appear in the feed as alerts (shown with a red indicator) so they're easy to spot. If you contact support, the Activity feed is a good place to note the last events and their timestamps.
